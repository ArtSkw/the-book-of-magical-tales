# Set Bible — "Dzwon z Księżycowego Wzgórza" / "The Bell of Moonhill"

Second tale, same pipeline as `SET_BIBLE_dragon-bridge.md` (continuity rules §0 there apply unchanged). Style: A v2 (`prototypes/prompts/_style-a2.txt`). Tone anchors: the dragon tale's approved pages until this tale has its own approved pages; the location sheet is layout only.

## 1. Cast (canon)

Gustaw and Oliwia: as in `MANUSCRIPT_BIBLE.md` §2.2–2.3 (red kite shield with a golden **griffin**).

- **Koniczynka** — Gustaw's mare: a gentle cream-white palfrey (`#ece2cc`) with a flaxen mane and tail, soft grey muzzle, dark kind eyes; a red caparison cloth (`#9b2f2a`) with a narrow gold border over her back, brown leather bridle. Calm, curious, often pointing her nose at things. Never a warhorse, never armoured.
- **The reeve (wójt)** — man of about fifty, short grey-brown beard, round felt hat (`#5b4a3a`), green wool coat to the knee (`#4f6b45`), a big iron key on his belt.
- **The baker (piekarz)** — man of about forty, round and cheerful, clean-shaven, white cap, cream shirt, white apron dusted with flour; often carries a basket of oat rolls.
- **The carpenter (cieśla)** — man of about thirty-five, long brown hair tied back, brown beard, rust-brown tunic (`#8a4f2e`), leather apron, rolled sleeves; carries the ash ladder.
- **The blacksmith (kowalka)** — woman of about thirty, strong arms, dark hair in a braid wrapped round her head, sooty leather apron over a slate-blue dress (`#4d5f73`), a small hammer at her belt.
- **Grandmother Janina** — old woman, small and bent, white kerchief, brown shawl over a grey dress, walking stick, bright eyes.
- **The finches** — a mother chaffinch (rosy-brown breast, blue-grey cap) and three fluffy chicks in a small cup nest of moss and grass.
- **Villagers** — a few families with horn lanterns, wool shawls; children.

## 2. The place (canon)

- **Compass:** the village sits on a gentle hill; **the square** is at the top. The **bell tower** stands on the **north** side of the square. The **old pear orchard** lies **behind the tower (north)**, reached by a path round the tower's **east** side. Mountains far away to the **north-west**.
- **Default camera:** in the square, south of the tower, looking north at the tower's front.
- **The bell tower:** a slender square tower, two-thirds stone (grey, rough-cut) and a timber-framed belfry on top with **four open arched windows, one per side**, a steep red-tiled pyramid roof with a small iron weathervane (a crescent moon). One wooden door at the foot of the south face, studded with iron. On the **north face**, high up at the belfry, one **wooden shutter** (green-grey) — the one the rope slipped through.
- **The bell:** bronze, warm brown-gold, the size of a barrel, hanging from a thick oak beam (the yoke) in the belfry; one bell rope.
- **The pear tree:** one old, wide pear tree just north of the tower, its biggest branch reaching toward the north shutter. Small green-gold pears. The **finch nest** sits in the fork of that branch, a hand's width from where the rope tangles.
- **The square:** cobbled, roughly round, a stone well in the middle, timber-framed houses with steep thatched and tiled roofs and small square windows around it, smoke from chimneys. The bakery is on the **east** side (a pretzel-shaped wooden sign — blank, no letters).
- **Inside the belfry:** wooden floor of planks, the oak beam, the four arched windows showing the night sky and the moon, a ladder hatch in the floor.

## 3. Story state

- Pages 1–2: evening, the bell silent; the rope missing from the square side (it hangs out of the north shutter into the pear tree).
- Page 3 onward: the rope is visible tangled in the pear tree; from page 5 it is free and hangs inside the tower again.
- Page 6: the hair-thin crack on the bell's rim, on the side facing the **east** window.
- Page 7: mended — a short row of **three small shining rivets** across the crack (`invite-smith` variant shows the blacksmith working them; both variants end with the rivets).
- Pages 6–10: one night, one moon: a **thin crescent** (as on the location sheet). It shows in the belfry windows on 6–7, rises over the tower on 8 and rides high on 9–10. Never a full moon.
- Pages 8–10: the bell has its rivets.
