# Seed — "Leśna przysięga" / "The Forest Oath" (tale three)

Status: draft for the author's approval (2026-10-31). Concept from a Fable brainstorm, chosen by the
author with the theme moved from mending to **keeping one's word when it costs**. Rules:
`docs/WRITING_BIBLE.md` (§3 spine and tale card, §4 journey). Ribbon: weld (yellow-green ochre),
embroidered star (`src/book/tales.js`, `tale-three`).

## 1. The spine

1. **The problem.** Long ago the people of Dąbrówka made an oath with the forest: the Oak Corner
   (Dębowy Kąt), the oldest oaks, belongs to the deer; people take only fallen wood from it. In
   return, the stag with a star between its antlers leads anyone lost in the autumn mist out of
   Dębowy Bór — if they stand still and wait. This autumn the oaks gave few acorns (a poor mast
   year, a real thing). A storm broke the Corner's fence, the swineherd boy Marcin's piglets went
   in and ate the deer's acorns, and the deer moved deeper into the forest. The oath is broken,
   though nobody meant to break it, and in the morning mist the stag does not come.
2. **Why it matters, and to whom.** To the deer: they need acorns to last the winter before the
   first frost. To Marcin: he is scared and ashamed, and his family's pigs also need food for the
   winter. To the travellers and Marcin, lost in the mist: without the stag there is no way out
   until it lifts. To the village: an oath that nobody keeps is just words.
3. **Why the heroes act.** Oliwia notices the clues (empty acorn cups, piglets' hoofprints, a
   broken post with a carved star) and understands three things: the pigs were hungry, not bad;
   Marcin kept quiet from fear, not malice; the deer went away because their food was gone. Gustaw
   is a knight: he knows that a given word is kept, and he shows it by what he does, not by a
   speech. They help because they can see what each one needs.
4. **The values shown.** Keeping one's word when it costs (giving the deer acorns in a year when
   there are few); honesty without fear (Marcin tells the truth, nobody shames him); understanding
   and compassion for everyone's needs (deer, pigs, a boy); respect for the forest and its
   animals (the Corner is theirs); cooperation (the fence, the acorns, the mare carrying sacks).
5. **The one thing to remember.** *We keep our word, even when it is hard, most of all to those
   who cannot remind us of it.* The last page says it in the world's own words: the star carved on
   the Corner's new fence post, and a saying of Dąbrówka (draft: „Gwiazdka na słupku przypomina,
   że las też ma nasze słowo”).

**Colour script** (new for the book: it ends in full daylight): p1–3 pre-dawn mist, pearl-grey and
blue; p4–6 dawn, the mist thinning, warm light in soft patches (never rays as lines); p7–8 late
morning, mist in patches, ochre leaves; p9 bright noon, golden oaks; p10 closing emblem.

**Curiosity notes** (checked facts): pigs were driven into oak woods in autumn to eat acorns;
a charcoal kiln (mielerz) smoulders under earth for days without flames; stags shed their antlers
every year and grow new ones; oaks have "mast years", with many acorns one year and few the next.

## 4. Risks for the pictures

- Antlers and deer legs: one stag in front, the herd as quiet silhouettes behind.
- The canon forbids fire, smoke threads and light rays: the kiln is an earth mound with no smoke;
  sunlight comes as warm patches on the ground and the mist as flat bands (as in the dragon tale).
- The stag's star: a small gold four-pointed star between the antlers, the same in every picture.
- Mist must stay readable: three figures and the piglets always clear; faces visible.
