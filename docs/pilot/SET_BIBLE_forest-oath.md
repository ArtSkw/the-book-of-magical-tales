# Set Bible — "Leśna przysięga" / "The Forest Oath"

Third tale, same pipeline as `SET_BIBLE_dragon-bridge.md` (continuity rules §0 there apply unchanged)
and `SET_BIBLE_moonhill-bell.md`. Story source: `SEED_forest-oath.md` (spine, tale card, beats,
colour script). Style: A v2 (`prototypes/prompts/_style-a2.txt`). Builder:
`prototypes/prompts/_build_forest.py` (prefix `fo-`). Sheets: `prototypes/refs/set-forest-oath.png`
(layout only) and `prototypes/refs/m-sheet-forest.png` (new cast).

New for the book: the tale goes from mist to full daylight. The canon still forbids a black night,
fire, smoke threads, light rays as lines and candles; here that also means **the charcoal kiln never
smokes** and **the mist is flat pale bands**, never a wall that hides a figure.

## 1. Cast (canon)

Gustaw and Oliwia: `MANUSCRIPT_BIBLE.md` §2.2–2.3 (Oliwia's leather satchel always across her chest).
Koniczynka: `SET_BIBLE_moonhill-bell.md` §1 (cream-white palfrey, flaxen mane, red caparison with a
gold border). She walks with them the whole tale: she is in every wide picture; in close shots she
may be out of frame.

- **Dobrochna** — the charcoal burner (węglarka), a sturdy woman of about fifty: weathered, kind,
  practical face with laugh lines, grey-streaked dark hair under a rust-brown kerchief (`#9a5a35`)
  tied at the nape; slate-grey wool dress (`#5d6268`) with sleeves rolled to the elbow; a long
  dark-brown leather apron (`#6b4a33`) with **soot smudges on the apron only** (her face and hands
  are clean, rosy cheeks); brown boots. Her one tool: a long-handled wooden rake. Never a lantern,
  never a torch.
- **Marcin** — the swineherd boy, about ten, a little taller than Oliwia (his head reaches Gustaw's
  shoulder): short dark-brown hair in a ragged bowl cut, freckles, big honest eyes; an oatmeal linen
  shirt under a weld-yellow ochre wool tunic (`#b59a3c`) belted with a cord; a short brown hood with a
  shoulder cape (`#7a5a3c`), often pushed back; brown hose patched at one knee; soft leather shoes.
  He carries one thin hazel switch (never used to hit). From p7 (`give-sack`): **his hemp sack of acorns**
  (`#c8b48a`, tied with a cord).
- **The three piglets** — always exactly three, real little pigs the size of a cat, curly tails,
  forward-flopping ears, small kind eyes, never clothed, never standing like people. Told apart by
  spots: **Big** — pink (`#e3a99a`) with one large dark saddle patch (`#3a2e2a`) over its back;
  **Middle** — pink with many small dark spots; **Smallest** — all pink with one dark ear.
- **The star stag** — a red deer stag, calm and gentle: warm red-brown coat (`#8a5a3a`), a darker
  shaggy neck mane, a pale cream rump; dark, kind, lashed eyes (never glowing); shoulder as high as
  Gustaw's shoulder. Antlers pale bone-ochre (`#d8c08a`), symmetrical, five points each side. **The
  star:** one small flat gold-leaf **four-pointed star** (`#c9972b`, crisp ink edge, the size of a
  child's palm) on its brow between the bases of the antlers, the same in every picture. It is a
  gold blaze, not a light: never a halo, rays, sparkles or glow; nothing else on the stag shines.
- **The doe and the fawn** — a red deer hind (no antlers, same red-brown, slimmer) and her small
  fawn (knee-high to Gustaw, big ears, a few faint pale spots fading on its back).
- **The herd** — two or three more hinds, only as quiet pale silhouettes in the mist (p8) or grazing
  small in the distance (p9). No other stags.
- No other people, no dogs, no hunters, no birds in the scenes (silence is the clock).

## 2. The place (canon)

- **Compass:** Dębowy Bór, an old October oak forest north of the village Dąbrówka (never drawn).
  **Dobrochna's charcoal clearing** is in the south of the forest. **The path** leaves the clearing
  at its **north** edge, between two great oaks, and runs north a few hundred steps to **the Oak
  Corner**, arriving at its **south fence**. **The deer trail** comes from the deep forest in the
  **north-east** and reaches the Corner's **east** side.
- **Default camera:** at the clearing's south edge, looking north: the hut at the left (west), the
  kiln in the centre-left, the woodpiles at the right (east), the path into the forest right of
  centre between the two great oaks.
- **The charcoal clearing:** a round, mossy clearing ringed by oaks. **The kiln (mielerz):** a low
  round dome of dark earth and turf sods, as tall as Gustaw and as wide as three people lying end to
  end, with a ring of small vent holes plugged with turf; **no smoke, no steam, no glow, no flame**,
  ever. **The hut:** small and low, of split logs with a turf roof, one plank door, one small square
  window, at the west edge. **The cart:** a two-wheeled wooden cart with wicker sides and a few full
  charcoal sacks, standing in front of the hut, shafts down (not yet harnessed). **Woodpiles:** neat
  stacks of oak billets at the east edge. A wooden water bucket by the hut door.
- **The forest:** big old oaks with round, cloud-like crowns of ochre, russet and olive leaves, a
  few hornbeams and birches; moss, brown bracken, fallen leaves and acorn cups underfoot. Friendly
  and open, never dark or tangled. No conifers, no poplars, no dead trees.
- **The Oak Corner (Dębowy Kąt):** a roughly square enclosure of **five** ancient oaks with huge
  trunks (three people could not hug one) and wide golden-ochre crowns, the ground inside soft moss.
  Round it a **low fence** of split oak: **two horizontal rails** between squared oak **posts** with
  rounded tops, waist-high to Gustaw (too high for piglets, easy for a deer to step over). **Every
  post** carries a **carved four-pointed star** near its top on the face that looks out (a slim,
  pointed compass-rose star, never a cross), weathered grey like the post.
- **The storm damage (south fence, facing the path):** one post **leans** sideways with its old
  carved star toward the path; the next post is **snapped** at the base; two rails lie in the grass;
  the gap between them is where the piglets went in.
- **The fallen branch:** a big storm-broken oak branch with twigs and brown leaves lying **across the
  deer trail** just east of the Corner, as high as a fawn's back. It matters only on p8–9.

## 3. Story state

- p1–p3: pre-dawn mist; no birds; rime on the grass tips (the first frost is coming). The clue on
  p1: empty acorn cups and small cloven hoofprints in the moss, leading out of the mist.
- p3–p5: the Corner's ground is strewn with **empty acorn cups**, almost no acorns. The south fence
  is broken (§2) until p5.
- p5 on: Gustaw has set the **leaning post upright**; its old star faces the path. The snapped post
  and the fallen rails stay as they are until p7.
- p6: the decision. **No sack in the picture**: Marcin's hemp sack of acorns (his family's winter
  food for the pigs) is still in his hut. Frost whitens the grass (the clock). The piglets nose empty
  cups and find nothing; they never eat acorns in a picture.
- p7 `give-sack`: the sack, fetched from the hut, rides on Koniczynka's back.
- p7 `give-sack`: Koniczynka carries the sack to the Corner; acorns are poured inside; Dobrochna adds
  a small basket of her own. `gather-forest`: everyone gathers acorns from the forest floor into the
  sack, baskets and Oliwia's satchel flap; the piglets sniff them out. **On both paths:** Gustaw and
  Dobrochna fit two new rails; the snapped post is replaced by a **new pale post** (fresh-cut,
  lighter wood) on which Marcin carves a **new star**. From p7 on the fence is whole: old grey posts,
  one new pale post with a fresh star, and acorns on the moss inside.
- p8: the stag comes out of the mist from the north-east along the deer trail, the herd behind it;
  the fawn is behind the fallen branch, its mother beside the stag.
- p9 `make-way`: the herd grazes inside the Corner; `help-fawn`: the branch has been lifted aside
  (it lies along the trail, not across), the fawn beside its mother. On both, the stag walks the
  path south toward the clearing and the mist rolls back from it; everyone follows hand in hand.
- Keepsake: from p9 Oliwia has **one acorn in its cup**, put into her satchel (same on both paths).
- p10: noon at the clearing; the closing emblem.

Mist rule (review 2026-10-31, the text: the forest „tonął w mlecznej mgle” until the stag returns):
on p1–p7 the mist is the main atmosphere — the far trunks fade into milky pearl-white, no sky, no sun,
no cast shadows, muted leaf colours — while the foreground figures stay crisp with readable faces.
The builder puts this `ATMOSPHERE` block first in the p1–p6 prompts; `fo-p1c`, once accepted, is the
mist reference for the other pages.

## 5. Layouts and shots

Plates (5:4, generated 1536×1024): the choice pages **p2, p4, p6, p8** and the pages with a "Did you
know" note in the text, **p1, p5, p7, p9** (kiln, pannage, mast years, antlers). Portrait (2:3): **p3**
only. (Corrected 2026-10-31: the first plan had p5, p7, p9 as portraits; their first takes were
portraits and are regenerated as plates.) p10: the closing emblem (a gold roundel, the pair
of the title emblem). Variants share their first variant's shot and camera.

Rhythm on both paths: WIDE → LOW → THRESHOLD → CLOSE → LOW → CLOSE at the post → MEDIUM group → FOLLOWING → WIDE →
emblem. No two consecutive pages share a shot; p1 and p9 rhyme (the same look north up the path,
mist then noon).
