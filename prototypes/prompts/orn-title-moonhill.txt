STYLE: marginal decoration from a late-medieval illuminated manuscript, drawn exactly in the manner of the margins in the game Pentiment (see the attached Pentiment page references: the berry vines, flower tendrils, birds and small beasts around the miniatures). Fine, confident near-black brown ink line (#2b1d14) with delicate hairline tendrils and tiny curls; light transparent watercolour washes in a restrained, muted palette (sage and olive greens, madder red berries, soft cornflower blue, ochre, warm browns), with natural mottling; small touches of burnished gold leaf as dots or tiny leaves (#c9972b). Airy and SUBTLE: thin stems, lots of empty space between motifs, nothing heavy or crowded — it must sit quietly beside text on a page and never compete with the main illustration. Same tone as the attached tone references. Never photographic, never 3D, never digital-airbrush. Absolutely no text, letters, numbers or signatures.
FORMAT: TRANSPARENT BACKGROUND (alpha channel, PNG). Only the ink-and-wash motif is opaque; everything else — including the paper — is fully transparent: no parchment colour, no background, no frame, no shadow.

MOTIF: the frontispiece emblem that opens the tale "The Bell of Moonhill" — an illuminated roundel like a precious manuscript initial: a circular medallion framed by a double ring of burnished gold leaf with a fine punched-dot border; inside, on a deep muted blue ground (#2f4a73) scattered with tiny gold stars and a gold crescent moon, the slender village bell tower of the tale (match the attached location sheet: grey stone base, timber-framed belfry with round arches, red-tiled pyramid roof with a crescent-moon weathervane) with the warm bronze bell visible in its arch, and beside it a branch of the old pear tree with small green-gold pears and a tiny finch nest with a mother chaffinch. Around the roundel, growing out of it to all sides like the margins of an illuminated page: delicate ink vine tendrils with pear blossoms, small pears, ivy leaves, a few tiny moths and gold-leaf dots and curls, balanced but hand-drawn and lively. Precious, enchanting, the feeling of opening a magical tale.

REFERENCE ROLES: the Pentiment pages define the line, wash, palette and lightness of the marginalia; the tone reference defines colour balance; never copy a motif exactly.
