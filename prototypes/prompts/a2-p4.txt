STYLE — OPTION A v2, "Pentiment manuscript": drawn exactly in the manner of the game Pentiment (see the attached Pentiment references: the character style sheet on parchment, Andreas in the red cap, the farmyard scene in the book frame), for a premium children's picture book (ages 4–8).
LINE: a confident, near-black brown ink line (#2b1d14) of slightly varying weight around every shape, like a dip pen: bold on figures, thinner in the distance. A few economical interior lines (fold lines, fingers, eyelids) — not dense hatching.
COLOUR: a SIMPLE, RESTRAINED, MUTED palette — about eight to ten colours for the whole picture, slightly desaturated and earthy, like mineral pigments on old paper: warm parchment cream, ochre, brick red, muted sage and olive greens, grey-olive stone, soft slate blue-grey, warm browns, the characters' fixed colours. Each shape is filled with ONE transparent watercolour wash of its local colour, with natural mottling, pigment granulation and soft blooms inside the shape, and at most one darker wash for its shadow side. Warm parchment paper grain shows through everywhere. No saturated orange sunset, no glowing sky, no rich gradients, no glossy highlights, no dramatic lighting — light is calm and even, the time of day is suggested only by a pale sky wash.
SIMPLIFICATION: like Pentiment, backgrounds are simplified and calm: trees as rounded cluster shapes with a few leaf marks, grass as a flat wash with a few strokes, hills as flat bands, sky as one pale flat wash with a few simple cloud shapes. Detail is concentrated on the characters and the key object; the rest stays quiet and readable. Still a finished, carefully composed illustration — never empty, never sketchy.
FIGURES: Pentiment proportions and faces — simple, warm, expressive; small dark eyes with a clear upper lid, simple straight noses, rosy cheeks as a soft wash; never anime, never big shiny eyes, never caricature.
GOLD: burnished gold leaf as flat ochre-gold (#c9972b) with a slightly brighter band and a crisp ink edge, used sparingly for magic and lantern light.
Perspective is believable and logical but lightly flattened like a medieval miniature. Never photographic, never 3D-render, never digital painting with soft airbrushed shading. Absolutely no text, letters, numbers, runes or signatures anywhere.

CHARACTERS (match the attached sheets for identity):
The dragon — copper-coloured (#c8742c) with lighter copper-orange belly plates (#e39a4f); about the size of a horse. A rounded, soft snout with small nostrils and a closed mouth with a gentle smile line; large almond eyes with heavy upper lids and dark-brown irises; two long pale striped horns (#d8c08a) swept back from the crown and a second, shorter pale pair in front of them; a ridge of short copper spines from the crown down the neck; small wings folded flat on the back; four-toed paws with pale claws. Expression: a worried, kindly grandfather. Never teeth, flames, red eyes or spread wings.
Sir Gustaw — a young knight of about twenty: chin-length wavy blond hair, no beard, long oval face. Grey mail shirt to mid-thigh over grey hose, grey knee plates, tall brown boots, sword sheathed at the left hip. Red cloak (#8a3a2c) clasped at the right shoulder with a round gold brooch. Red kite shield (#9b2f2a) with a golden rampant griffin, slung on his back. A bronze lantern with horn panes, a carrying ring and one candle inside.
Oliwia — a girl of about nine, two heads shorter than Gustaw: long wavy chestnut-brown hair past her waist with a thin braid around the crown, ankle-length teal-green dress (#3f7d78) with narrow gold trim at neck, cuffs and hem, a brown leather satchel on a strap across her chest, brown ankle boots. Small tan hands with short fingers.
The three river mice (always exactly three, one huddled group): Big — grey-brown (#8b7b66), upright ears, long tail; Middle — warm tawny brown (#a3805a), slimmer, white tip to its tail; Smallest — pale sandy (#c9a97e), round as a hazelnut, oversized pink ears, pink nose, stubby tail. Cream bellies, dark bead eyes, fine whiskers. Real mice — no clothes, never standing like people.

SET — the old bridge (must match the attached location sheet exactly): a narrow humpbacked packhorse bridge of rough grey-olive stone with exactly ONE semicircular arch over a shallow clear brook with pebbles. The deck is cobbled. Low parapets on both sides, knee-high to the knight, built of rough blocks topped with broad flat mossy capstones with a few tiny white flowers. Trailing ivy on the downstream face left of the arch. At each end the parapets flare out into a low dry-stone wall. Reeds along both banks; an old oak at the west end. Central European countryside: oak, alder, willow, birch — never cypresses.
SET — the nest: the loose stone is one capstone of the downstream parapet on the east half of the bridge, slightly tilted, darker and thick with moss; along its inner edge (the deck side) runs one narrow mossy crack, two fingers wide — the mice's only door.

CAMERA: we stand on the cobbled deck of the bridge, on its east half, just behind Oliwia, looking steeply down over her shoulder at the downstream parapet. The parapet runs across the picture from left (toward the crown, west) to right (east); beyond it, below, the brook with mist.
SCENE (page 4, "A Plan for the Little Mice", a cold misty dusk): Oliwia kneels on the cobbles in the foreground at the bottom-left, seen from behind and slightly above: her long chestnut hair with the thin braid around the crown, her teal shoulder and the satchel strap. The knee-high parapet is in front of her, its top at the height of her chest. In the middle of the picture lies the loose capstone — tilted, darker, thick with moss — and along its inner edge, facing us, the narrow mossy crack; peeking out of the crack, huddled together, exactly three river mice, whiskers trembling, looking up. Oliwia's two small hands rest half a loaf of dark rye bread on a square of cream linen on the capstone beside the crack, very gently. On the left, Sir Gustaw kneels on one knee on the cobbles by the parapet, face visible from above, calm and careful, holding the bronze lantern low and still above the crack by its ring; its warm light falls on the loose stone and the mice. On the right, the dragon lies along the deck, its big copper head lowered onto the capstones, chin resting on the parapet, snout near the crack, heavy-lidded eye kind and careful; from its nostrils a small soft warm golden breath drifts toward the mice. Beyond the parapet, below, wisps of white mist rise from the brook. Everyone is perfectly still; a moment of held breath. The mice are the clearest thing in the picture; all three gifts point at them.
TEXT IT ILLUSTRATES: "The evening grew colder and mist rose over the stream like a white ribbon. In the mossy crack the mice trembled their whiskers. Gustaw held a lantern, Oliwia half a rye loaf wrapped in linen, and the dragon breathed warmth more gently than a candle."
COUNTS: exactly three mice; one lantern; one half loaf on one linen; one dragon; one Gustaw; one Oliwia (from behind). No other people or animals.
NEGATIVES: no text; no fourth mouse; no fire or flames; nobody sitting or kneeling on top of the parapet; no second crack; not a flat map; no saturated colours.

QUALITY: clear focal point readable at a glance by a 5-year-old; detail concentrated around the focal point; calm simplified background; the image fills the canvas edge to edge, no border, no frame, no margin.
