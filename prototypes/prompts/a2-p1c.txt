STYLE — OPTION A v2, "Pentiment manuscript": drawn exactly in the manner of the game Pentiment (see the attached Pentiment references: the character style sheet on parchment, Andreas in the red cap, the farmyard scene in the book frame), for a premium children's picture book (ages 4–8).
LINE: a confident, near-black brown ink line (#2b1d14) of slightly varying weight around every shape, like a dip pen: bold on figures, thinner in the distance. A few economical interior lines (fold lines, fingers, eyelids) — not dense hatching.
COLOUR: a SIMPLE, RESTRAINED, MUTED palette — about eight to ten colours for the whole picture, slightly desaturated and earthy, like mineral pigments on old paper: warm parchment cream, ochre, brick red, muted sage and olive greens, grey-olive stone, soft slate blue-grey, warm browns, the characters' fixed colours. Each shape is filled with ONE transparent watercolour wash of its local colour, with natural mottling, pigment granulation and soft blooms inside the shape, and at most one darker wash for its shadow side. Warm parchment paper grain shows through everywhere. No saturated orange sunset, no glowing sky, no rich gradients, no glossy highlights, no dramatic lighting — light is calm and even, the time of day is suggested only by a pale sky wash.
SIMPLIFICATION: like Pentiment, backgrounds are simplified and calm: trees as rounded cluster shapes with a few leaf marks, grass as a flat wash with a few strokes, hills as flat bands, sky as one pale flat wash with a few simple cloud shapes. Detail is concentrated on the characters and the key object; the rest stays quiet and readable. Still a finished, carefully composed illustration — never empty, never sketchy.
FIGURES: Pentiment proportions and faces — simple, warm, expressive; small dark eyes with a clear upper lid, simple straight noses, rosy cheeks as a soft wash; never anime, never big shiny eyes, never caricature.
TONE CONSISTENCY: the whole book shares the colour balance of the attached TONE REFERENCES (two approved pages of this book): natural, gently warm, balanced — muted earthy colours, but not washed out or faded. The time of day (given in the scene) only makes the picture a little darker and cooler; it never adds sunset oranges or pinks, glowing skies or a different palette.
GOLD: burnished gold leaf as flat ochre-gold (#c9972b) with a slightly brighter band and a crisp ink edge, used sparingly for magic and lantern light.
Perspective is believable and logical but lightly flattened like a medieval miniature. Never photographic, never 3D-render, never digital painting with soft airbrushed shading. Absolutely no text, letters, numbers, runes or signatures anywhere.

CHARACTERS (match the attached sheets for identity):
Sir Gustaw — a young knight of about twenty: chin-length wavy blond hair, no beard, long oval face. Grey mail shirt to mid-thigh over grey hose, grey knee plates, tall brown boots, a sword always sheathed in a brown scabbard at his left hip. Red cloak (#8a3a2c) clasped at the right shoulder with a round gold brooch. Red kite shield (#9b2f2a) with a golden rampant griffin.
Oliwia — a girl of about nine, two heads shorter than Gustaw (her head reaches his chest): long wavy chestnut-brown hair past her waist with a thin braid around the crown, dark brows, oval face; ankle-length teal-green dress (#3f7d78) with narrow gold trim at neck, cuffs and hem; a brown leather satchel on a strap across her chest; brown ankle boots; small tan hands.
The dragon — copper-coloured (#c8742c) with lighter copper-orange belly plates (#e39a4f); about the size of a horse: lying down, its back reaches a grown man's waist and its head is longer than a man's torso. A rounded soft snout with small nostrils and a closed mouth with a gentle smile line; almond eyes with heavy upper lids and dark-brown irises; two long pale striped horns (#d8c08a) swept back and a second, shorter pale pair in front; a ridge of short copper spines down the neck; small wings always folded flat on its back; a long plain tail; four-toed paws with pale claws. A worried, kindly grandfather. Never teeth, flames, red eyes or spread wings.

SET — the old bridge (must match the attached location sheet exactly): a narrow humpbacked packhorse bridge of rough grey-olive stone with exactly ONE semicircular arch over a shallow clear brook with pebbles. The deck is cobbled. Low parapets on both sides, knee-high to the knight, built of rough blocks topped with broad flat mossy capstones with a few tiny white flowers. Trailing ivy on the downstream face left of the arch. At each end the parapets flare out into a low dry-stone wall along the bank. Reeds along both banks; an old oak at the west end. Central European countryside: oak, alder, willow, birch — never cypresses.
SET — the nest: the loose stone is one capstone of the downstream parapet on the east half of the bridge (the third capstone east of the crown), slightly tilted, darker and thick with moss; along its inner edge (the deck side) runs one narrow mossy crack, two fingers wide — the mice's only door. Inside the parapet, under that capstone, is their nest.
SET — east bank: beyond the east end the road climbs a low rounded hill to a small village of whitewashed houses with steep thatched roofs and small square windows, one squat stone church tower with a short wooden spire, and at the top of the road the inn: two storeys, timber-framed, a blank hanging wooden sign, three small square windows on the upper floor. Upstream on the east bank a small watermill: stone ground floor, timber upper floor, thatched roof, wooden water wheel.
LANDSCAPE (same in every picture of this tale): the brook is shallow, clear and lively, running over round pebbles between firm grassy banks with clumps of reeds; beyond it gentle green meadows, single alders, willows and oaks, low rolling hills — the friendly countryside of the tone reference with the helpers walking onto the bridge. Never a marsh, swamp, bog, pond or still murky water; no fog banks or mist hiding the land; no dead trees, no cypresses or poplars; nothing gloomy.
TIME (colour script): early evening; low sun from the west; pale cream sky at the horizon fading to pale grey-blue above, a few small soft clouds. Same tone as the tone references, a touch warmer.

CAMERA: the default view — we stand on the downstream (south) bank a little way from the bridge, looking north at its downstream face. West is on the left, east on the right; the brook flows toward us out of the one arch. Portrait framing: the brook and reeds in the foreground at the bottom, the whole bridge across the middle, the village small on its hill at the upper right, the sky above.

SCENE (page 1, "An Unexpected Meeting"): toward evening, Sir Gustaw and Oliwia have just arrived on the road at the west (left) end of the bridge, under the old oak, and stopped: Gustaw a step ahead with his shield on his arm, Oliwia beside him holding her satchel strap, both looking with quiet surprise toward the middle of the bridge. There, at the crown of the bridge, sits a small copper dragon — small for a dragon, about the size of a horse — sitting upright ON THE COBBLED DECK, BEHIND the near (downstream) parapet, in the calm, dignified pose of a guardian: like a stone lion at a gate — chest up, neck raised, head held level and turned toward the travellers, both front paws planted firmly side by side on the capstone in front of it. From our side view the knee-high parapet hides its hind feet and the lower part of its legs, and its body rises above the parapet from the hips up. Its paws are NOT raised, not folded, not pleading — it is keeping watch, not asking for anything. Its long tail comes over the parapet a little east of the crown and wraps around the one loose, mossy capstone, as if guarding something very fragile. Its face is watchful and quietly worried under heavy lids, never frightening, never pleading. Moss soft as a green blanket grows between the stones; the brook whispers under the arch; reeds at the water's edge.

TEXT IT ILLUSTRATES: "Toward evening, Sir Gustaw and little Oliwia stood before an old bridge… Moss grew between the stones, soft as a green blanket; a brook whispered beneath the arches; and there, in the very middle, sat a small copper dragon. To their surprise, it looked deeply worried. It did not roar. It only held its paws beneath its chin and wrapped its tail around one loose stone, as if guarding something very fragile."
